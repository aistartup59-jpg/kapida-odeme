# ADR-015: Two Products, One App — Business Sign-In and Login-Free Platform Hand-Off

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-08-01
- **Extends:** ADR-005, ADR-013

## Context

The same app serves two audiences whose relationship to us is completely different.

A small business is our customer. They sign up, sign in, key an amount in, and collect. An account is the right model: it is theirs, it persists, and it is how they see their own money.

A courier delivering for an order platform (Uber Eats, Getir, Yemeksepeti, Trendyol Go) is not our customer and never will be. They work inside the platform's driver app, may take one cash-on-delivery order a week, and change employer without telling anyone. Making them hold an account would mean onboarding thousands of people we have no relationship with, and would put a credential on a device we do not manage, for an app they open once.

So the courier must not sign in at all: tap "collect payment" in the platform's app, land in ours, take the money, go back.

That leaves the question an account normally answers — on whose behalf, and for how much? A deep link is opened by another app on the courier's device, and anything it carries is a claim made by software on a phone. If the amount travelled in the link, any app on that device could open a collection for any merchant, for any figure.

## Decision

### The business side

Sign-up and sign-in in the app. A business owner registers themselves (`POST /auth/merchant/register`), or staff they invited sign in as employees. They key the amount in by hand. Nothing here changes.

### The hand-off side

Authorisation is a **hand-off token**, minted by the only party that can be trusted with the amount: the platform's own backend, which holds the merchant's API key.

1. The platform's backend calls `POST /partner/handoffs` with `{externalOrderId, totalAmount}` and its API key. We open the PaymentRequest and return a token.
2. The platform's app deep-links into ours with nothing but that token:
   `payals://collect?token=hof_<publicId>_<secret>&returnUrl=<platform link>`
3. The courier collects. `GET /handoff/payment`, `POST /handoff/payment/qr` and `POST /handoff/payment/transactions` are authorised by the token alone.
4. Once the payment is PAID, the app returns the courier to `returnUrl`.
5. The platform's backend confirms server side with `GET /partner/payments?externalOrderId=...` before letting the courier mark the order delivered.

The token is scoped to exactly one PaymentRequest and expires in four hours. **No endpoint in the hand-off channel takes a payment id** — the token names the payment, so there is no id for a caller to substitute and no way to reach another order.

`externalOrderId` is unique per merchant, so minting twice for one order returns the PaymentRequest that already exists rather than charging the customer twice.

`externalOrderId` is **not** accepted on `POST /payments`. It belongs to the channel where a platform backend authorised the amount; letting a signed-in client set it would let them attach themselves to an order they do not own (ADR-009).

### Why no authorization rule changed

A hand-off PaymentRequest has no `employeeId` — no courier account exists to own it. That does not make it an ownerless order sitting in someone's queue: nobody browses for it, because the token reaches it directly. Employee scoping is untouched, and the business owner still sees these payments in their own merchant-wide list, which is what they need for reconciliation.

## Consequences

- Couriers install the app and collect. There is no onboarding, no password, and no credential worth stealing beyond a single collection that is about to happen anyway.
- The integration costs the platform one backend call per order. That call is the whole security model: it is what makes the amount something a server said rather than something a phone claimed.
- The integration is platform-neutral. Nothing in the backend or the app names a platform.
- Hybrid payments survive the hand-off — part cash, part bank QR — and the platform sees the full breakdown (ADR-002, ADR-013).
- Payment completion is observed by polling. Pushing it to the platform (a signed webhook with retries) is deliberately left to a later sprint rather than half-built here.
- A platform that cannot make a server-side call before dispatch cannot integrate. That is the intended trade: the alternative is accepting amounts stated by an app on a courier's phone.

## Alternatives Considered

- **Courier accounts.** Rejected: thousands of people we have no relationship with, onboarding friction at exactly the wrong moment, and a long-lived credential on an unmanaged device.
- **Platform-created orders that any courier picks up.** Rejected: it needs ownerless PaymentRequests visible to every employee, changing an authorization rule the product deliberately locked.
- **Amount carried in the deep link, no token.** Rejected. Any app on the device could then open a collection for any merchant, for any amount.
- **A device credential provisioned once per courier phone.** Rejected: a long-lived secret on an unmanaged device, and the amount would still come from the device.
- **Trusting the deep-link result as proof of payment.** Rejected. It is a claim made by software on the courier's device about money.
- **Uber Eats-specific endpoints.** Rejected. Uber Eats has not operated in Turkey since 2021, and a platform-shaped integration would have to be rewritten for whichever platform is integrated first.
